# Endgames Overview

The **endgame** is where knowing a few exact techniques pays off directly — many
games are won or drawn by a single well-known method. This section covers specific,
named endgames you can learn and reuse.

If you haven't yet, start with the fundamentals on the
[Endgame Basics](/docs/chess-rules/endgame-basics) page — the opposition, king and
pawn vs. king, and the rule of the square. The techniques here build on those ideas.

## King & Pawn Endgames

The foundation of all endgames — almost every ending can simplify into one:

- **[King & Pawn Endgames](./king-and-pawn)** — key squares and triangulation, the
  ideas that decide who promotes.

## Rook Endgames

Rook endgames are the most common of all, so these two positions are the most
valuable to memorize:

- **[The Lucena Position](./lucena-position)** — the key *winning* technique when you
  have an extra pawn ("building a bridge").
- **[The Philidor Position](./philidor-position)** — the key *drawing* technique when
  you are defending a pawn down.

Learn Lucena to convert a win, and Philidor to save a draw — together they decide a
huge share of practical rook endgames.
