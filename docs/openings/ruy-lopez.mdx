# Ruy López

The **Ruy López** (also called the **Spanish**) is one of the oldest and most
respected openings. Like the [Italian Game](./italian-game), White develops
naturally — but instead of aiming the bishop at f7, White uses it to pressure the
knight that defends Black's `e5` pawn.

## Main Line

```
1. e4 e5
2. Nf3 Nc6
3. Bb5
```

## Key Ideas

- **Pressure the defender.** The bishop on b5 pins the knight on c6 against the e5
  pawn, indirectly attacking Black's center.
- **The threat is positional, not immediate.** White isn't winning the pawn yet
  (after `Bxc6 dxc6`, `Nxe5` gives the pawn back to `Qd4` or `Qg5`), but the tension
  shapes the whole game.
- **Long-term play.** The Ruy López often leads to rich, strategic middlegames where
  small advantages matter — which is why it's a favorite at every level.

## Typical Continuation

The most common reply is **3... a6**, the **Morphy Defense**, asking the bishop to
decide. Step through the line, or press **Play**:

<MoveViewer moves="1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bb5 a6 4. Ba4 Nf6 5. O-O" />

White retreats the bishop (keeping the pin) and castles. This is the starting point
of countless famous games.

:::tip
The Ruy López rewards understanding over memorization. Focus on the idea — pressure
on `c6` and `e5` — rather than trying to recall long move sequences.
:::
