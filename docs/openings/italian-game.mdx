# Italian Game

The **Italian Game** is one of the oldest and most natural openings. It's an
excellent first opening: every move follows the
[opening principles](/docs/chess-rules/opening-principles) — control the center,
develop a piece, and prepare to castle.

## Main Line

```
1. e4 e5
2. Nf3 Nc6
3. Bc4
```

## Key Ideas

- **Fight for the center.** White's pawn on e4 and Black's on e5 stake out the
  middle of the board.
- **Quick development.** Both knights come out early (`Nf3` / `Nc6`), and White's
  bishop reaches the active `c4` square in one move.
- **The bishop eyes f7.** The bishop on c4 points straight at f7 — Black's weakest
  square, defended only by the king — so watch for tactics there.
- **Castle soon.** After a move like `Bc5` or `Be7` for Black, both sides usually
  castle kingside within a couple of moves.

## Typical Continuation

A very common way for Black to reply is the symmetrical **Giuoco Piano** ("quiet
game"). Use the controls to step through the moves — or press **Play** to watch the
whole line:

<MoveViewer moves="1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4 Bc5 4. c3 Nf6 5. d4" />

White builds a big pawn center while both sides finish developing.

:::tip
The Italian is a great choice for beginners because the plans are straightforward:
develop, castle, and aim at f7. Play through the main line on the board a few times
until the moves feel natural.
:::
