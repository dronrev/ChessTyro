# Check, Checkmate & Stalemate

The king is the most important piece. These three situations all revolve around
the safety of the king.

## Check

A king is **in check** when it is attacked by at least one enemy piece. When you
are in check, you **must** get out of it on your very next move. There are three
ways to respond:

1. **Move** the king to a safe square.
2. **Block** the attack by placing a piece between the king and the attacker
   (not possible against a knight or an adjacent attacker).
3. **Capture** the piece giving check.

You may **never** make a move that leaves your own king in check.

## Checkmate

**Checkmate** occurs when a king is in check and there is **no legal move** to
escape it. The game ends immediately, and the checkmated player **loses**.
Checkmate is the primary way to win a game of chess.

## Stalemate

**Stalemate** occurs when the player to move has **no legal move** but their king
is **not in check**. This is a **draw** - neither player wins.

Stalemate is a crucial defensive resource: a losing player may sometimes save the
game by forcing a stalemate. Be careful not to stalemate an opponent when you are
trying to checkmate them.

could move to (a7, b7, b8) is covered by the white queen - so with no legal move,

:::tip Check vs. Stalemate
The key difference: in **checkmate** the king *is* under attack with no escape; in
**stalemate** the king is *not* under attack, but the player simply has no legal
move.
:::
