# How the Pieces Move

Each type of piece moves in its own way. A piece may **capture** an enemy piece by
moving onto its square (the captured piece is removed from the board). You can
never move onto a square occupied by your own piece, and — except for the knight —
pieces cannot jump over other pieces.

:::tip
On the boards below, **click any piece to see the squares it can move to** (shown
as dots). Give it a try!
:::

## King

Moves **one square in any direction** — horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.
The king can never move into check (a square attacked by an enemy piece). See
[Special Moves](./special-moves) for castling.

## Queen

The most powerful piece. Moves **any number of squares** in a straight line —
horizontally, vertically, or diagonally — until blocked.

<InteractiveBoard fen="k7/8/8/8/3Q4/8/8/7K" turn="w" highlight="d4" />

## Rook

Moves **any number of squares horizontally or vertically**. Also participates in
castling.

<InteractiveBoard fen="k7/8/8/8/3R4/8/8/7K" turn="w" highlight="d4" />

## Bishop

Moves **any number of squares diagonally**. Each bishop stays on its starting
color for the entire game.

<InteractiveBoard fen="k7/8/8/8/3B4/8/7P/7K" turn="w" highlight="d4" />

## Knight

Moves in an **"L" shape**: two squares in one direction and then one square
perpendicular. The knight is the **only piece that can jump over** other pieces.
It always lands on a square of the opposite color to the one it left.

## Pawn

The pawn is the most restricted piece, and its movement and capture are different:

- **Forward move:** one square straight forward (never backward).
- **First move:** from its starting square, a pawn may advance **one or two
  squares**.
- **Capture:** one square **diagonally forward**, never straight ahead.
- Pawns also have two special rules: **[en passant](./special-moves#en-passant)**
  and **[promotion](./special-moves#promotion)**.
