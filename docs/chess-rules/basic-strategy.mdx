# Basic Strategy

Once the pieces are developed, the **middlegame** begins. Strategy is about
long-term planning - improving your position little by little rather than looking
only for immediate captures.

## Control the Center

Pieces placed in or aimed at the center are more powerful because they influence
more squares. Two pawns abreast on **d4 and e4** give White a strong "classical
center" and more space to maneuver:

## Piece Activity

A well-placed piece is worth more than a passive one. Aim to:

- Put **knights** on strong central squares where they can't be chased away by
  pawns (an *outpost*).
- Give **bishops** open diagonals; a pair of bishops is a real asset in open
  positions.
- Place **rooks** on **open files** (columns with no pawns) so they can penetrate
  the opponent's position.

## King Safety

Keep your king protected - usually castled, with the pawns in front of it intact.
Avoid pushing those pawns without reason, as each advance creates weaknesses around
your king. In the **endgame**, when few pieces remain, the king becomes a fighting
piece and should be brought toward the center.

## Pawn Structure

Pawns can't move backward, so pawn moves are permanent. Watch out for structural
weaknesses:

- **Doubled pawns** - two of your pawns on the same file; they can't defend each
  other.
- **Isolated pawn** - a pawn with no friendly pawns on adjacent files; it must be
  defended by pieces.
- **Backward pawn** - a pawn that has fallen behind its neighbors and can't safely
  advance.
- **Passed pawn** - a pawn with no enemy pawns able to stop it from promoting;
  these are very valuable, especially in the endgame.

## Making a Plan

Good moves come from a plan, not random attacks. A simple thinking routine:

1. **Look for checks, captures, and threats** - yours and your opponent's.
2. **Find your worst-placed piece** and improve it.
3. **Identify weaknesses** in your opponent's camp to target.
4. **Make sure every piece is doing a job** before starting an attack.

:::tip
When you don't see a tactic, improve a piece. Chess games are often decided by who
has more active pieces working together.
:::
