# Opening Principles

The **opening** is the first phase of the game, where both players develop their
pieces and fight for the center. You don't need to memorize long move sequences —
following a few sound principles will get you a healthy position.

## The Core Principles

1. **Control the center.** The four central squares (d4, e4, d5, e5) are the most
   valuable. Pawns and pieces in the center control more of the board.
2. **Develop your pieces.** Bring your knights and bishops off the back rank early,
   toward active squares. **Knights before bishops** is a useful guideline.
3. **Castle early.** Get your king to safety and connect your rooks — usually
   within the first ten moves.
4. **Don't move the same piece twice** in the opening without a good reason; every
   move should develop something new.
5. **Don't bring your queen out too early.** It can be chased around by the
   opponent's minor pieces, costing you time (*tempo*).

## Common Openings

Openings are named sequences of opening moves. Here are three of the most famous.

### Italian Game

Moves: **1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4**. White develops naturally and aims the
bishop at Black's vulnerable f7 square.

### Ruy López (Spanish)

Moves: **1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bb5**. One of the oldest and most respected
openings; the bishop pressures the knight that defends Black's e5 pawn.

### Sicilian Defense

Moves: **1. e4 c5**. Black's most popular reply to 1. e4. Instead of mirroring
White, Black fights for the center asymmetrically and plays for a win.

:::tip
In the opening, count your **developed pieces**. If your opponent has more pieces
active and a safer king, they are usually better — even if material is equal.
:::
