# Endgame Basics

The **endgame** is the final phase, when few pieces remain. Here the king becomes a
strong attacker, and even a single extra pawn is often enough to win. These
fundamentals turn small advantages into full points.

## The Opposition

When the two kings stand on the same file (or rank) with **one empty square between
them**, they are in **opposition**. The player who does *not* have to move holds the
opposition - the other king must step aside and give ground.

Here the kings face off on the e-file. Whoever is **not** to move controls the key
squares:

Winning the opposition lets your king force its way forward - the key to most
king-and-pawn endgames.

## King & Pawn vs. King

A lone pawn only promotes if your **king leads it** and controls the squares ahead.
With the king in front of the pawn and the opposition, White wins:

<InteractiveBoard fen="4k3/8/4K3/4P3/8/8/8/8" turn="w" />

If instead the defending king sits **directly in front** of the pawn with the
opposition, the game is usually a **draw** - the attacker can't make progress
without allowing stalemate or losing the pawn.

## The Rule of the Square

To see whether a lone king can catch a running passed pawn, imagine the **square**
whose side runs from the pawn to its promotion rank. If the defending king can step
**into that square** on its move, it catches the pawn; if not, the pawn promotes.

The white pawn on a5 needs three moves to reach a8. The black king on e5 is
**outside** the square - if it's White's move the pawn runs and queens:

<InteractiveBoard fen="8/8/8/P3k3/8/8/8/7K" turn="w" />

:::tip
Two rules of thumb for endgames:
1. **Activate your king** - bring it toward the center and into the action.
2. **Push passed pawns**, but only with your king supporting them.
:::
