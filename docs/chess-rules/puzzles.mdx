# Puzzles

Test yourself! In each position it is **White to move**. Work out the best move and
**play it on the board** to check it, then click to reveal the answer. (Squares are
named with [algebraic notation](./notation).)

## Puzzle 1 - Mate in one

White to move and checkmate.

<InteractiveBoard fen="6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/8/3R2K1" />

<details>
  <summary>Show the answer</summary>

  **Rd8#.** The rook drops to the back rank and delivers a classic **back-rank
  mate**. The black king is trapped behind its own pawns on f7, g7, and h7, and
  can't escape.

</details>

## Puzzle 2 - Win the queen

White to move and win material with a **fork**.

<InteractiveBoard fen="4k3/8/8/3N3q/8/8/8/4K3" />

<details>
  <summary>Show the answer</summary>

  **Nf6+.** The knight jumps to f6 with check, attacking the king on e8 **and** the
  queen on h5 at the same time. After the king moves out of check, White plays
  **Nxh5**, winning the queen.

</details>

## Puzzle 3 - Finish the attack

White to move and checkmate.

<InteractiveBoard fen="3qk3/3ppp2/8/7Q/2B5/8/8/6K1" />

<details>
  <summary>Show the answer</summary>

  **Qxf7#.** The queen captures on f7, protected by the bishop on c4. The black king
  can't take the queen (the bishop defends it) and has no escape - its own queen and
  pawns block every flight square. This is the pattern behind the famous
  *Scholar's Mate*.

</details>

:::tip
Stuck? Run through your **checks, captures, and threats** first - the answer to a
puzzle is almost always a forcing move.
:::
