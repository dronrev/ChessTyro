# Draws & Other Endings

Not every game ends in a win. A game can end in a **draw** (a tie) in several ways.

## Ways a Game Is Drawn

- **Stalemate** — the player to move has no legal move but is not in check (see
  [Check, Checkmate & Stalemate](./check-checkmate-stalemate#stalemate)).
- **Agreement** — both players simply agree to a draw.
- **Threefold repetition** — the **same position** occurs three times (with the
  same player to move and the same possible moves); either player may claim a draw.
- **Fifty-move rule** — if **50 consecutive moves** by each player pass with no
  pawn moved and no capture made, either player may claim a draw.
- **Insufficient material** — neither side has enough pieces to deliver checkmate,
  for example:
  - King vs. King
  - King and Bishop vs. King
  - King and Knight vs. King
- **Dead position** — no sequence of legal moves could ever lead to checkmate.

## Other Ways a Game Ends

- **Resignation** — a player who believes the position is hopeless may **resign**,
  giving the win to the opponent. This is common in serious play.
- **Timeout (flag fall)** — in timed games, a player who runs out of time loses,
  unless the opponent has insufficient material to checkmate, in which case it is a
  draw.

## Relative Value of the Pieces

While not an official rule, these approximate point values help you judge whether a
trade or capture is worthwhile:

| Piece  | Value (points) |
| ------ | -------------- |
| Pawn   | 1              |
| Knight | 3              |
| Bishop | 3              |
| Rook   | 5              |
| Queen  | 9              |
| King   | Priceless (cannot be captured) |
