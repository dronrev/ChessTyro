# Basic Tactics

**Tactics** are short forcing sequences that win material or deliver checkmate. Most
games between beginners are decided by tactics, so learning to spot these patterns
is the fastest way to improve.

## Fork

A **fork** is a single piece attacking **two or more** enemy pieces at once. Knights
are famous for forking because they attack in a way no other piece can block.

Here the white knight on c7 gives check to the king on e8 **and** attacks the rook
on a8. The king must move, and White wins the rook:

## Pin

A **pin** freezes an enemy piece: it can't move because a more valuable piece sits
behind it. In an **absolute pin** the piece behind is the king (moving would be
illegal); otherwise it is a **relative pin**.

The white bishop on g5 pins the black knight on f6 to the queen on d8 - if the
knight moves, White wins the queen:

<InteractiveBoard fen="3q3k/8/5n2/6B1/8/8/8/4K3" turn="b" />

## Skewer

A **skewer** is like a pin in reverse: the **more valuable** piece is in front and
is forced to move, exposing the piece behind it.

The white rook on e1 checks the black king on e4. When the king steps aside, the
rook captures the queen on e7:

## Discovered Attack

A **discovered attack** happens when you move one piece out of the way to unleash an
attack from a piece behind it. When the moving piece also creates its own threat,
these can be devastating.

If the white knight on e5 moves, the rook on e1 attacks the black queen on e8 - and
the knight can create a second threat as it leaves (for example a knight check):

<InteractiveBoard fen="k3q3/8/8/4N3/8/8/8/4R1K1" turn="w" />

:::tip
Before every move, scan for **checks, captures, and threats** - both yours and your
opponent's. Most tactics are found by asking "what are all my forcing moves?"
:::
