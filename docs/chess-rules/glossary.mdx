# Glossary

A quick reference of common chess terms used throughout this guide.

- **Blunder** - a very bad move that loses material or the game (written `??`).
- **Castling** - moving the king two squares toward a rook and the rook to the
  other side; see [Special Moves](./special-moves#castling).
- **Check** - an attack on the king that must be answered immediately; see
  [Check](./check-checkmate-stalemate#check).
- **Checkmate** - a check with no legal escape; the game ends.
- **Development** - bringing your pieces off their starting squares into active play.
- **En passant** - a special pawn capture; see
  [Special Moves](./special-moves#en-passant).
- **En prise** - a piece left undefended and open to capture "for free".
- **Fianchetto** - developing a bishop to the long diagonal (e.g. g2 or b2).
- **File** - a column of the board, labeled a–h.
- **Fork** - one piece attacking two or more enemy pieces at once; see
  [Tactics](./tactics#fork).
- **Gambit** - sacrificing material (usually a pawn) early for quick development or
  attack.
- **Initiative** - the ability to make threats and force the opponent to react.
- **Material** - the pieces and pawns; being "up material" means having more.
- **Opposition** - kings facing off with one square between; see
  [Endgame Basics](./endgame-basics#the-opposition).
- **Outpost** - a strong square for a piece (often a knight) that enemy pawns can't
  attack.
- **Passed pawn** - a pawn with no enemy pawns able to stop it from promoting.
- **Pin** - freezing a piece because a more valuable one sits behind it; see
  [Tactics](./tactics#pin).
- **Promotion** - turning a pawn that reaches the far rank into another piece; see
  [Special Moves](./special-moves#promotion).
- **Rank** - a row of the board, numbered 1–8.
- **Sacrifice** - giving up material on purpose for a bigger gain.
- **Skewer** - like a pin, but the more valuable piece is in front; see
  [Tactics](./tactics#skewer).
- **Stalemate** - no legal move while *not* in check; the game is a draw.
- **Tempo** - a unit of time (one move); "gaining a tempo" means developing while
  making a threat.
- **Zugzwang** - a position where any move a player makes worsens their position.
- **Zwischenzug** - an "in-between move": an unexpected move (often a check) inserted
  before an expected recapture.
