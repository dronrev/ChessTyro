# Special Moves

Beyond the basic piece movements, chess has three special moves that every player
must know.

## Castling

Castling is the only move where you move **two pieces at once** — the king and one
rook. It helps get the king to safety and develops a rook.

To castle, move the **king two squares toward a rook**, then place that **rook on
the square the king crossed over**.

- **Kingside (short) castling:** king moves toward the nearer rook.
- **Queenside (long) castling:** king moves toward the farther rook.

Castling is only allowed when **all** of these are true:

1. The king and the chosen rook have **never moved**.
2. There are **no pieces between** the king and that rook.
3. The king is **not currently in check**.
4. The king does **not pass through or land on** a square attacked by an enemy
   piece.

e1 two squares to g1** — the rook on h1 automatically jumps to f1:

## En Passant

A special pawn capture. If your opponent advances a pawn **two squares** from its
starting position and it lands **directly beside one of your pawns**, you may
capture it **as if it had only moved one square**.

- The capture is made by moving your pawn **diagonally forward** to the empty
  square the enemy pawn skipped over.
- It must be done **immediately** — on the very next move — or the right is lost.

**e5**. White may capture *en passant* by moving to d6:

## Promotion

When a pawn reaches the **far side of the board** (the opponent's back rank), it
must be **promoted** — immediately replaced by a **queen, rook, bishop, or knight**
of the same color.

- You may choose any of these pieces regardless of what has been captured, so it
  is possible to have, for example, **two queens**.
- Promoting to a queen (the strongest piece) is by far the most common choice and
  is called *queening*.
